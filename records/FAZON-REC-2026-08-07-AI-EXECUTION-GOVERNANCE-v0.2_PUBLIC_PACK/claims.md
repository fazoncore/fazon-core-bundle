# Supported Public Claims and Non-Claims

## Supported public claims

1. AI governance is broader than action-specific permission.
2. Capability does not independently establish authority.
3. Identity and access do not independently establish action-specific permission.
4. A valid policy or governance package may be an authoritative input within its declared scope, but does not automatically authorize every later proposal.
5. Point-in-time evidence does not automatically establish current action permission.
6. Monitoring, incident reporting, audit, and receipts are important controls but do not independently make the pre-action permission decision.
7. The FAZON public category position is:
   `Enterprise AI Security and Governance → AI Execution Governance → Pre-Execution Action Admissibility`.
8. The governed object is the specific proposed action.
9. The decision moment is before an external or binding consequence.

## Explicit non-claims

This record does not claim:

- an adopted industry taxonomy;
- ownership of generic terms such as AI governance, execution governance, runtime governance, permission, or admissibility;
- first, only, unique, certified, compliant, production-ready, or universally applicable status;
- legal advice or regulatory conformity;
- public conformance, certification, endorsement, partnership, integration, or implementation equivalence;
- that a receipt alone proves authorization;
- that every non-permit outcome is technically guaranteed to prevent every side effect;
- disclosure of FAZON internal mechanisms.
